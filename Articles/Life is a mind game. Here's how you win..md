# Life is a mind game. Here's how you win.

At some point, usually in your 20s, you’ll notice that the people around you stop believing in themselves. And no matter how hard you try, you can’t save them. By all means, do not let it infect your mind. Stay on your path.

I was mulling over that thought a few days ago. And the more I think about it, the deeper the problem goes. I can’t help but notice that too many people between the ages of 20 and 35 fall into the blatantly obvious vicious cycle:

1. You get motivated by some external source (a video, a book, a conversation).
    
2. You feel like this is your moment. You go all in.
    
3. You start the business, the workout, or the skill.
    
4. But like clockwork, 2 days or 2 weeks later... you fall off.
    

You go back to the life you swore you despised. As if the desire to change was just another form of **cheap dopamine**.

---

## Your mind is a collection of survival strategies

Survival is the mechanical, often unconscious, engine behind every thought. There are two types of survival:

1. **Physical survival:** Reproducing genes (largely solved in the modern world).
    
2. **Psychological/conceptual survival:** Reproducing the information in our consciousness—our ideas, beliefs, and worldview.
    

We have **memes** (units of culture that spread from mind to mind). Your identity is a composition of memes that create a psychological structure. Religions, nations, and even your morning routine are "religions" or survival structures for these memes.

When you try to pursue a goal that fundamentally changes who you are, your ego throws out defensive reactions. Your mind floods with anxiety because your identity feels threatened. **Every thought you have centers around the survival of your self-image.**

---

## You can’t escape the mind game, but you can master it

Effortless self-discipline happens when the desire to become the highest version of yourself outweighs the desires of the lowest version.

- **Losers** are afraid of success (it threatens their current identity).
    
- **Winners** are afraid of mediocrity.
    

To flip the script, you must weaponize your survival mechanism so you feel threatened by _failure_ rather than _growth_.

### 1 – Find a reason with high gravitational pull

Discover it by breaking out of the familiar past. If you wake up and immediately think of your stressful commute, you are bringing the past into the present. Only in the unknown will you find the insight to launch into the next phase.

### 2 – Become brutally aware of who you don’t want to become

Write down exactly where your life will end up if you keep doing the same thing. If the answer doesn't disgust you, you aren't being honest. Your current identity has a survival interest in "softening the blow" so it doesn't have to change.

### 3 – Change your environment faster than the identity can recalibrate

Your identity is stored in your surroundings—your phone, your room, your friends.

- Wipe your phone.
    
- Spend a week somewhere new (even an Airbnb nearby).
    
- Immerse yourself in the environment of your future self.
    

### 4 – Increase the gap between impulse and response

This is the only way to interrupt the survival mechanism in real time. When the urge to go back to old habits hits, **do nothing**. Starve the old self of the feedback it craves. Train your nervous system to tolerate the gap between impulse and action.

---

## Winning the game is how you discover it’s the wrong game

The goal is to work with **tremendous intensity** on things that matter, yet remain **strangely unbothered** when they don't work out.

Many successful people simply replace one source of suffering with another (e.g., the millionaire who is still unhappy because their identity is tied to the next goal). You must play the survival game to grow, but you must also transcend it.

> **Distinguish pain from suffering.** > Pain is a feature of life. Suffering is the identity’s refusal to accept the event.

The mark of a mature mind is being a contradiction: fearing mediocrity enough to work hard, but being unbothered enough not to collapse when things fail.
